The problem

AI is making investment decisions no one can explain.

A black box, even to the people who built it.

AI in investing produces recommendations without a clear account of how it got there.

No one can reconstruct the reasoning.

When something goes wrong, teams are left guessing at what the model saw and why it acted.

Risk committees can't approve what they can't see.

Compliance and oversight teams are being asked to sign off on decisions with no evidence trail behind them.

What LoQOS does

Seven commitments, not seven features.

What you get and what it feels like to use it, in plain terms.

01

Every decision, on the record.

Replay any decision months later, exactly as it was made: the evidence, the reasoning, the checks it passed, and what the approver saw. When someone asks "why did you do that?", you have the answer, not a reconstruction.

02

AI proposes. Rules decide.

The AI can suggest anything. It cannot act outside the limits you set, and it cannot change those limits. A wrong suggestion becomes a rejected proposal, not a trade.

03

Evidence you can check.

Every claim points to the exact sentence in the exact source it came from. If the source doesn't say it, it doesn't get in.

04

Honest about uncertainty.

When the system is less sure, it commits less. It tells you what it doesn't know instead of hiding it behind a confident number.

05

Know what every decision cost.

The research spend behind each decision is attached to it, so you can see whether your AI is earning its keep.

06

Human oversight you can measure.

Most systems claim a human is in the loop. We measure whether the review is actually working, so oversight is a number, not a promise.

07

Built for the hard questions.

Designed from day one for the questions your investors, auditors and regulators will ask about AI.

What it looks like to use

Three moments with LoQOS.

The morning

A ten-minute brief shows what happened overnight, what it cost, what was flagged, and what needs your attention.

A decision

A proposal shows the recommendation, how confident the system is, the evidence quoted from the source, and anything that argued against it. You approve or reject in minutes.

Six months later

An auditor asks about one decision. You replay it exactly as it happened.
