ResearchNote 04

Five properties that separate a guardrail from a blocker.

048 Oct 2026

LocusQuant3 minute read

Most AI controls in finance are blockers. They sit in front of an action and say no. That keeps the worst outcomes out, but it teaches an agent nothing, so the agent tries the next nearby thing, and the next, until something gets through or the work stalls. A guardrail does more. It keeps the agent safe and keeps it useful. Five properties separate the two.

1. It bounds outcomes, not every step

Limiting each step forces approvals everywhere and slows everything. Limiting where the agent may end up, such as the exposure it may hold or the money it may spend, leaves it free in how it gets there.

2. Every denial explains itself

A bare “no” invites guessing. A denial that says which limit applied, and what would be allowed instead, lets the agent re-plan on the first try.

3. A safe path is always open

Whatever binds, there must be a way to make things smaller and safer. An agent boxed in with no exit is a risk of its own.

4. It is proven by trying to break it

A rule nobody has attacked is a hope. Every guardrail should come with a record of the attempts made to get past it, and what happened.

5. Its cost is measured

Every limit costs something in missed opportunity. When that cost is measured rather than guessed, the people who set limits can tighten or loosen them with the trade-off in view.
